Key points from reference and source are connected

A transformation function, the homography, is computed from the matched points.

  • A homography is a 3×3 matrix that maps source positions onto the reference.
  • The image is split into small cells, and each cell gets its own homography, for example:

Filters the field of homographies instead of the matches.

  • Bad matches corrupt a few cells, and their homographies cause ghosting in the warp.
  • Each of the 9 entries is replaced by its median over a (2k+1)×(2k+1) window of neighbouring cells:
  • Outliers end up at the ends of the sorted list, so the median never picks them.
  • Replaces RANSAC: no matches are thrown away before fitting.
  • k is measured in cells, not pixels.

Pixels are projected through the homography transformation function.

  • Each pixel of the aligned image is traced back through its cell's homography to a position in the source image.
  • It takes the colour found there, so the source lands exactly on the reference.
